Eight features, and that is the whole product. Anything you don't find here
isn't hidden — it's on the [roadmap](/docs/engineering/roadmap) with a version
number against it.

## Setting up

<Accordions type="multiple">
  <Accordion title="Sign in">
    Authenticate to reach your roadmap(s). A roadmap always belongs to exactly
    one account.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Create a roadmap">
    A roadmap is a named, time-boxed plan: a name, a start date, an end date,
    and the time of day you want the email to arrive.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Upload questions">
    Upload or curate the list of LeetCode problems that make up the roadmap.
    Each problem has a title, a link, and a difficulty.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Configure a roadmap">
    Name, dates and send time are all editable after the fact. Nothing about
    the schedule is precomputed, so an edit takes effect on the very next
    email — there is no regeneration step to wait for.
  </Accordion>
</Accordions>

## Day to day

<Accordions type="multiple">
  <Accordion title="The daily email">
    The centerpiece: one email per roadmap per day, carrying between one and
    five problems. See [The daily email](/docs/product/daily-email) for how
    that number is chosen and why it changes.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="View progress">
    See a roadmap's progress in-app: problems completed out of the total, and
    days elapsed out of the total.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Mark complete">
    Mark a problem as solved.

    <Callout type="info" title="One-directional, on purpose">
      Un-marking a problem isn't supported yet. Progress is recorded as an
      append-only history, so this is a scope choice rather than a technical
      limitation — adding "un-mark" later doesn't cost that history.
    </Callout>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Archive a roadmap">
    Archive a roadmap to stop it nagging you, and unarchive it later. An
    archived roadmap keeps all of its data — archiving deletes nothing.

    This is how a roadmap reaches a resting state: because the end date is a
    goal rather than a deadline, an unfinished roadmap would otherwise keep
    emailing you indefinitely.
  </Accordion>
</Accordions>
